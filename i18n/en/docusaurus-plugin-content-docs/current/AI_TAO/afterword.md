# Afterword: About What You Are Doing Right Now

Your reading to this point is itself a choice.

This book will not be recommended to you by an algorithm. It has no tautologies to trigger your emotions, no "learn it in three minutes" headlines to bait your clicks, no anxiety to drive your sharing. It asks you to spend several hours reading, asks you to follow the argument from the first chapter to the final one, asks you to make your own judgement after finishing — rather than being judged for.

Algorithms reward the intensity and speed of emotional reactions, not the depth of thought. So content like this has virtually no chance of being seen in a platform's organic distribution. Chapter 5 dismantled this structure: when the visibility of content is directly tied to its commercial value, in-depth analysis will always rank behind emotional clickbait.

But you saw it.

Not because the algorithm decided you needed to see it. You arrived here yourself. Perhaps someone sent it to you; perhaps you stumbled upon it in some corner; perhaps you read the Schopenhauer passage in the Prologue and decided to keep going. Regardless, you used your own judgement to decide whether to spend this time. That act itself is what this book is trying to defend — the capacity for independent judgement.

If you feel this book says things worth being seen by more people, then the most concrete thing you can do is pass it to someone you believe would understand.

Not for my sake.

Because the unit of transmission for a book has never been the like; it is the person. Every handoff says something to this algorithm-distributes-everything environment: some things, people choose for themselves.

Chapter 1 dismantled the distortion of information in transmission. But there is one mode of transmission that does not distort much — one person placing a book in another person's hands and saying: "Take a look at this." That action has no headline compression, no translation drift, no editorial angle. It has only one layer: I think this is worth your time; you judge for yourself.

Content that runs counter to algorithmic logic requires a mode of transmission that runs counter to algorithmic logic — relying on people, not machines.
