# Where the Shares Went: Foreign Investors Sold Over NT$2.5 Trillion, and Locals on Borrowed Money Took the Other Side

2026-11-11 · @Mythogen Engine

*A companion piece to the series AI in the Financial Statements | By @Mythogen Engine*

Recorded on the morning of November 11, 2026, five days before the filing deadline for third-quarter 2026 financial statements in Taiwan. The five articles in the series were built on a database of company filings. This one uses daily statistics from the exchange, the central bank and the Financial Supervisory Commission (FSC), plus public reporting. It is written before the results are known so that it can be checked afterwards. It offers no trading signals.

## On the day the series was dated, Taiwan's market set its largest intraday point drop on record

The five articles in AI in the Financial Statements are dated November 6. On the same day the TAIEX fell as much as 3,737.67 points intraday, the largest intraday point drop in its history. It closed down 1,873.08 points at 39,701.64 ([Yahoo Finance Taiwan](https://tw.stock.yahoo.com/news/%E5%8F%B0%E8%82%A1%E8%B7%8C%E5%A4%AA%E5%87%B6%EF%BC%81%E6%94%BF%E5%BA%9C%E5%87%BA%E6%89%8B%E3%80%8C%E6%B2%92%E6%94%B6%E7%A9%BA%E6%96%B9%E6%AD%A6%E5%99%A8%E3%80%8D-%E9%99%90%E7%A9%BA%E4%BB%A45%E5%A4%A7%E6%8B%9B%E7%99%BD%E8%A9%B1%E6%96%87%E4%B8%80%E6%AC%A1%E7%9C%8B-035500659.html)).

From the intraday high of 49,968 on October 6, the index lost 20.55% in one month. That evening the National Stabilization Fund was authorised to intervene for the tenth time, with a ceiling of NT$500 billion. The FSC announced that from November 9 the cap on intraday borrowed-share short sales would fall from 30% to 3%, and the minimum margin on short sales would rise from 90% to 130% ([Manager Today](https://www.managertoday.com.tw/articles/view/73282), [Economic Daily News](https://money.udn.com/money/story/5607/9863115)).

Over the next two sessions the index rebounded 3,010 points. It closed at 42,711.92 on November 10, recovering roughly three-tenths of the decline ([CNA](https://www.cna.com.tw/news/afe/202611100353.aspx)).

The series followed what happens inside companies: where the costs went. This piece follows what happens outside them: where the shares went. Part of the index has come back, but the people holding these shares are no longer the same people.

Before the results arrive, the market has already taken on a layer of key-person risk.

## Three layers of leverage are high at once, and the direction of rates has reversed

Year-end settlement happens every year. What differs this year is that the market enters it carrying three layers of leverage while the cost of borrowing is rising.

| Layer | Figure | Comparison |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Taiwan margin loans | Margin balance on the main board reached NT$646.4 billion on October 8, a record | Since July 30 the index rose 23.49% while margin loans rose 30.89% |
| US margin debt | FINRA margin debt hit a record US$1.502 trillion in June; US$1.48 trillion in September | 4.48% of GDP, against a long-run average of 3.09% |
| Corporate AI debt | AI-linked companies have issued US$466 billion of debt so far in 2026 | US$101 billion in the same period last year |

Sources: [Shin Kong Securities](https://www.skis.com.tw/Report/education/KNW20261012.html), [Finzdata](https://finzdata.com/research/margin-debt-leading-indicator), [GuruFocus](https://www.gurufocus.com/economic_indicators/4266/finra-investor-margin-debt-relative-to-gdp), [AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/hyperscaler-debt-insurance-hits-highest-cost-since-2021).

The third layer did not exist in earlier years. Roughly 35% of the large cloud providers' capital spending is now funded with debt, and Goldman Sachs analysts note that as a group these companies had negative free cash flow in the third quarter ([TheStreet Pro](https://pro.thestreet.com/market-commentary/windows-for-ai-debt-ipos-data-center-permits-appear-to-be-closing), [Briefs](https://www.briefs.co/news/debt-markets-warn-ai-data-center-builders-financing-is-getti/)). Credit markets have already responded: the yield on CoreWeave's 2032 bond has risen above 16% ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/06/coreweave-bond-yield-tops-16percent-as-ai-credit-stress-rises.html)).

This is the same story as the third article in the series, ["Where Did the Costs Go?"](https://mythogenengine.com/docs/finance/where-did-the-costs-go). That piece read it off the balance sheet. Now the bond market is putting a price on it.

Rates are the fourth condition. In most years the market goes into year-end hoping for cuts. This year it is pricing a December hike. High leverage alone does not have to cause trouble. High leverage with rising interest costs is where the pressure comes from.

Two corrections belong here. Taiwan's margin balance is a record in absolute terms, but as a share of total market value it was about 0.38% to 0.4% in June, far below the 4.2% to 4.6% of 2000 ([Wistock](https://blog.wistock.ai/stock-market-insights-main-player-trends/%E5%8F%B0%E8%82%A1%E8%9E%8D%E8%B3%87%E9%A4%98%E9%A1%8D%E8%A1%9D%E4%B8%8A5909%E5%84%84%E5%85%83%E5%89%B5%E6%96%B0%E9%AB%98-%E6%8F%AD%E9%9C%B2%E8%9E%8D%E8%B3%87%E6%A7%93%E6%A1%BF/)). Second, some of the leverage has already been cleared. The main-board margin balance fell to NT$576.4 billion on November 5, down 10.8% from the peak. On the same day the market-wide collateral maintenance ratio was 159.77%, with margin calls on 2,352 accounts and forced liquidation of 222 ([Shin Kong Securities](https://www.skis.com.tw/Report/education/KNW20261106-2.html), [CNA](https://tw.stock.yahoo.com/news/%E5%8F%B0%E8%82%A1%E5%A4%B1%E5%AE%884%E8%90%AC%E9%BB%9E-%E4%B8%8A%E5%B8%82%E6%AB%83%E8%9E%8D%E8%B3%87%E9%A4%98%E9%A1%8D%E5%90%88%E8%A8%88%E5%A4%A7%E6%B8%9B564-18%E5%84%84%E5%85%83-135005707.html)).

## Where the shares went: foreigners sold, locals bought, and some of those locals bought on borrowed money

Foreign investors have net sold about NT$2.55 trillion of Taiwan equities this year (as of November 3). That is a record and more than twice the 2022 figure. October alone saw NT$994.3 billion of net selling, the largest month on record ([United Daily News](https://udn.com/news/story/7238/9855519)).

The buyers are local, and they fall into three groups:

- **Investment trusts (domestic funds)**: NT$1.02 trillion of net buying this year, an annual record, and 21 consecutive sessions of net buying through November 10.
- **The eight state-owned banks**: NT$531.06 billion of net buying this year, also an annual record.
- **Margin accounts and holders of leveraged ETFs**: the margin balance is near its all-time high, and during the decline many retail investors bought the dip through highly leveraged index ETFs.

Sources: [Economic Daily News](https://money.udn.com/money/story/5607/9855518), [Yahoo Finance Taiwan / CNA](https://tw.stock.yahoo.com/news/%E5%A4%96%E8%B3%87%E9%80%A39%E8%B3%A3%E5%8F%B0%E8%82%A1%E7%BA%8C%E7%A0%8D341%E5%84%84%E5%85%83-%E9%80%A323%E8%B3%A3%E5%8F%B0%E7%A9%8D%E9%9B%BB-135203130.html), [Anue](https://news.cnyes.com/news/id/6648403).

Three numbers show that the rebound has not brought foreign investors back:

- On November 9 the index rose 1,960 points, yet the New Taiwan dollar weakened by NT$0.114 to close at 32.882 per US dollar. Foreign investors net sold about NT$90 billion that day.
- Through November 10, foreign investors had net sold for 9 consecutive sessions, NT$829.8 billion in total, and had net sold TSMC for 23 consecutive sessions.
- Foreign investors' net short position in TAIEX futures rose to 103,400 contracts.

Sources: [United Daily News](https://udn.com/news/story/7239/9867391), [CNA](https://www.cna.com.tw/news/afe/202611100353.aspx), [UAnalyze](https://uanalyze.com.tw/articles/9653358876).

When stocks rise while the currency falls, the rebound is being driven by local money and policy, and foreign investors are using the bid to keep leaving. The central bank governor told the legislature on November 10 that foreign investors had remitted more than US$10 billion out of Taiwan since the start of November ([Radio Taiwan International](https://tw.news.yahoo.com/%E5%A4%96%E8%B3%87%E8%B3%A3%E8%B6%85-%E5%8F%B0%E5%B9%A3%E7%BA%8C%E8%B2%B6-%E5%A4%AE%E8%A1%8C-11%E6%9C%88%E8%87%B3%E4%BB%8A%E5%B7%B2%E5%8C%AF%E5%87%BA%E9%80%BE%E7%99%BE%E5%84%84%E7%BE%8E%E5%85%83-031430672.html)).

Four points here are easy to confuse:

1. **Net selling is not the same as money leaving.** FSC data show that for the first ten months of the year, foreign investors were still net inward remitters of US$101.929 billion ([MoneyDJ](https://www.moneydj.com/kmdj/news/newsviewer.aspx?a=6a7d5bfc-e605-403d-955f-7acac3c666b0)).
2. **Only part has left.** Foreign investors still hold 47% of listed market value, about NT$69.57 trillion ([Commercial Times](https://www.ctee.com.tw/news/20261102701401-430201)). Their combined holdings of Taiwan stocks, bonds and NT dollar deposits total US$1.743 trillion, roughly three times the foreign exchange reserves ([Taipei Times](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/biz/archives/2026/11/07/2003867290), [CNA](https://www.cna.com.tw/news/afe/202611070037.aspx)).
3. **Some of the selling is mechanical.** Foreign investors have net sold about NT$2.1 trillion of TSMC this year, and some large institutions attribute it mainly to single-position size limits and not to fundamentals ([Economic Daily News](https://money.udn.com/money/story/5607/9652438)).
4. **Local money is not mainland Chinese money.** Official statistics group mainland capital with foreign capital. Mainland QDII funds face a US$500 million aggregate cap on money remitted in to invest in Taiwan securities, under a rule published in 2010 ([FSC law database](https://law.fsc.gov.tw/LawContent.aspx?id=FE220852)).

The judgement of this section fits in one sentence: foreign investors sell when they choose to, and margin accounts sell when they are called. The same shares now sit with holders who are easier to force out, so the next decline will bring a different kind of selling.

## Four views: the disagreement is about money flows, not fundamentals

On the actual numbers for AI demand, the camps differ little. They differ on whether the money will come back.

| Stance | Who | Argument |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Optimistic | [Morgan Stanley strategist Michael Wilson](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/wall-street-strategists-say-sp-500-correction-is-likely-nearing-an-end-5011797); [some local investment advisers](https://setn.com/news/1936704) | The correction is in its final phase, with S&P 500 downside to 7,100 to 7,300; TSMC's revenue shows demand is intact |
| Official | [Central bank](https://udn.com/news/story/7239/9868702); [FSC](https://www.cna.com.tw/news/afe/202611060301.aspx) | Foreign investors are reallocating after taking profits, and flows are normal; Taiwan's decline sits between those of other major Asian markets |
| Pessimistic | [Analyst Lien Chien-wen](https://finance.ettoday.net/news/3267272) | Margin positions have not been fully flushed out, and bad news in the filings will only be absorbed after November 16 |
| Sceptical | [An opposition legislator](https://tw.news.yahoo.com/%E5%A4%96%E8%B3%87%E8%B3%A3%E8%B6%85-%E5%8F%B0%E5%B9%A3%E7%BA%8C%E8%B2%B6-%E5%A4%AE%E8%A1%8C-11%E6%9C%88%E8%87%B3%E4%BB%8A%E5%B7%B2%E5%8C%AF%E5%87%BA%E9%80%BE%E7%99%BE%E5%84%84%E7%BE%8E%E5%85%83-031430672.html) | Volume is heavy on down days and thin on up days, while foreign investors keep selling and remitting out |

The optimists and the pessimists are using different evidence. One side looks at revenue and orders. The other looks at margin balances and foreign flows. Both sets of numbers are real.

The historical record argues against concluding too early. One count finds that of 14 corrections in which a single-day gain of more than 2% appeared, 9 gave back the entire bounce within a month. After similar breakdowns in the chip index, 7 cases went on to gain 14% on average and 6 turned into bear markets with a further average loss of 28% ([Gokhshtein](https://gokhshtein.com/news/2026-11-10-sp-500-rebounds-23-strategists-diverge-on-bottom)). That is a coin flip, not a signal.

November 16 and 17 are the nearest reveal, not the largest one. This decline has been driven by expectations of rate hikes, and that question is answered only when the Fed decides on December 9.

Three more dates concern fund flows. The MSCI changes take effect at the end of November (reports give both November 26 and November 30). Investment trusts tend to lock in positions in mid to late December ahead of annual performance rankings. Hedge funds close their year on December 31.

Two items are unconfirmed and are marked as such. Neither the arrangement nor the agenda of a US-China-Russia three-way meeting has been settled ([Korea Times](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/world/20261107/china-confirms-discussions-underway-on-historic-xi-trump-putin-meeting)). The date of the large AI listing appears only in media reports, and the company has not announced one. If the US-China talks touch on wording about Taiwan or on arms sales, the same headline may read one way for US stocks and the opposite way for Taiwan's.

Each of these dates is routine on its own. What differs this year is that the positions have passed to holders who are easier to force out, so every routine date becomes a test.

## Testable judgements: if three conditions appear together, this piece is wrong

The judgement here is that the change of holders is not finished, and that a rebound in the index is not the same as money returning. If the following three conditions appear together, that judgement has to be revised.

1. Foreign investors turn from consecutive net selling to consecutive net buying in the cash market, for at least five sessions.
2. Foreign investors' net short position in TAIEX futures falls clearly below 100,000 contracts. The baseline is 103,400 on November 10.
3. On days when Taiwan stocks rise, the New Taiwan dollar strengthens too. The baseline is November 9, when stocks rose and the currency fell.

There are two checkpoints: November 30, and after the Fed decision on December 9.

One further observation can be tracked continuously. If the index recovers while the main-board margin balance grows faster than the index, leverage is building again. The baselines are NT$646.4 billion on October 8 and NT$576.4 billion on November 5.

## Limits: what this piece cannot see or prove

- **Definitions differ.** Cumulative foreign net selling is NT$2.55 trillion in media tallies (as of November 3) and NT$2.29 trillion in FSC data that include the OTC market (as of end-October). For October net outward remittance by foreign investors, the central bank's figure including dividends is US$28 billion, and the FSC's figure is US$6.461 billion.
- **Some data cannot be seen.** Hedge-fund redemption volumes are not public. How much foreign selling is mechanical, and who ultimately funds money entering through third jurisdictions, cannot be separated out from public data.
- **"Locals on borrowed money" is shorthand.** The local buyers are investment trusts, state-owned banks and margin accounts. Only the last group buys with borrowed money, and the funds themselves do not borrow. No statistic shows how much each group absorbed.
- **Causation is not shown.** High leverage, rising rates and foreign selling occurred together. The data do not say which caused which. Taiwan's decline was close to Japan's and South Korea's, and nothing in it points to selling aimed at Taiwan alone.
- **The historical samples are small.** A comparison across 14 cases and another across 13 shows only that both outcomes have happened.
- **Timing.** Data run to the morning of November 11, and some figures may be revised. The QDII cap cited is from a rule published in 2010, and current rules were not separately verified.
- **The method differs from the five series articles.** This piece uses daily flow statistics and public reporting, not company-by-company financial statements.

This piece describes a structure, not price levels, and it is not investment advice.
