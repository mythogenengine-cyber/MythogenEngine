# Job Postings, Job Titles and the Actual Work: The Workforce Changes Hidden in AI Transformation (Trilogy Preface)

Nov 19, 2026 · @Mythogen Engine

In November 2026, a designer posted on Threads that the company had decided to shut down its entire design team. No one was laid off, but every designer was moved into a PM role.

Within a year, the poster wrote, the design workflow had changed completely: from full user research and Figma prototypes to almost everything generated by AI, with the designer left adjusting AI-generated interfaces. In the replies, the poster added two things. First, the company's design system was so thoroughly documented that AI had clear guidelines to follow, so AI-made design was "really not bad anymore." Second, one person now covers five or six products, each with three to five engineers, deciding priorities and keeping design consistent across them. It is a lot of work, the poster said, but many companies don't see its value.

Put those two remarks together and design work hasn't disappeared. It was packaged into guidelines for AI, and the remaining judgment is done by the same person under the title PM.

In the statistics, this will be recorded as fewer designers and more PMs. Readers of labour reports will conclude that AI replaced designers. What actually happened is one person doing two jobs under one title.

The three articles in this series look at the same thing from three angles:

1. "[A Job Posting Is Not Always a Hire: How Restructuring Merges Several Roles into One and Makes Everyone Reapply](/docs/notes/a-job-posting-is-not-always-a-hire)": several roles merged into one new posting; the data shows postings up and hires down. Merged roles mostly carry PM titles.
2. "[The JD Looks Great on Paper — The Reality of the 'Forward Deployed Engineer' Role](/docs/TechNotes/JD%20%E5%BE%88%E8%B1%90%E6%BB%BF%EF%BC%8C%E7%8F%BE%E5%AF%A6%E5%BE%88%E9%AA%A8%E6%84%9F%EF%BC%9A%E5%89%8D%E7%B7%9A%E9%83%A8%E7%BD%B2%E5%B7%A5%E7%A8%8B%E5%B8%AB%E9%80%99%E5%80%8B%E3%80%8C%E6%96%B0%E8%81%B7%E7%BC%BA%E3%80%8D)": one role absorbs several jobs, and the better it is done, the faster its own value is erased.
3. "[The People Who Built AI Were Laid Off, the People Who Promoted It Stayed: The Job-Title Game Behind Corporate "AI Talent Expansion"](/docs/notes/ai-title-game)": titles detach from the actual work. AI talent rises on the books while the company's own ability to build AI falls to zero.

The designer's situation falls into all three at once: the role was merged, the knowledge was handed to AI, and the new title no longer says what the work is.

As you read, keep asking one question: are we counting job titles, or the work people do?
